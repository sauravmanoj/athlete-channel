# 5 mistakes killing your running stamina
Promise: fix the 5 mistakes that leave you gasping early in a run, so you can run longer, feel better and keep improving week after week.
Framework: five mistakes — too fast, too much too soon, overstriding, no recovery, underfuelled.

## Hook
You set off fast. 4 minutes later, you're bent over, gasping, wondering how anyone runs for an hour.
The problem usually isn't your lungs. It's a few mistakes you can fix this week.
Today: the 5 mistakes killing your running stamina, and the fix for each one.

## The five mistakes
Mistake 1: running every run too fast. Mistake 2: too much, too soon. Mistake 3: overstriding. Mistake 4: no recovery days. Mistake 5: running underfuelled.
The first one is why you gasp early. The rest are why you stop improving.

## Mistake 1: Every run too fast
Your body has a redline speed. Below it, your breathing settles and you can keep going for a long time. [S9]
Above it, you're spending a small, fixed reserve. The further over the line you go, the faster it empties. When it's gone, you have to slow right down. [S9]
Think of your phone on full brightness with every app open. Fine for 10 minutes. Dead by lunch. Easy pace is battery-saver mode.
And the best endurance athletes do most of their training easy: around 70 to 80% of it. [S4]
The fix is the talk test. If you can speak comfortably in full sentences, you're at or below your first breathing threshold, which is well under your redline. [S7][S8][S9]
If you can only get out 2 or 3 words, slow down, or walk. [S7][S8]
Every 5 minutes or so, say a sentence out loud. A line from a song works.
New to running? Use run-walk. The NHS Couch to 5K plan starts with 8 one-minute runs, with 90 seconds of walking in between. [S13]
Walking breaks are part of the official plan. They're not cheating. [S13]
The mistake within the fix? Easy pace that creeps faster, until you're racing your mate or your watch. Let your breathing set the pace.

## Mistake 2: Too much, too soon
Your muscles get stronger fast. Your tendons take longer to catch up. [S26]
In one review, beginners got injured more than twice as often per hour of running as recreational runners. [S3]
And injury stops all progress.
Here's the surprise. In a study of over 5,000 runners, the weekly increase wasn't linked to injury. One sudden long run was. [S2]
A run 10 to 30% longer than your longest in the last 30 days came with about 1.6 times the overuse-injury rate. More than doubling it came with about 2.3 times. [S2]
Think of building a wall. Your muscles lay bricks fast. The cement needs time to set.
So the fix is the spike check. Before any run, ask: is this longer than my longest run this month? If it is, make it only a little longer: no more than about 10%, and less is fine. That's a sensible ceiling, not a proven safe limit. [S2]
If you're coming back after a break, start from your current longest run, not the one you used to do.
Starting from zero? Couch to 5K takes 9 weeks, 3 runs a week, and you can repeat any week that felt too hard. [S13]
The mistake within the fix? Keeping a sensible weekly total, then cramming it into one huge weekend run. [S2]

## Mistake 3: Overstriding
Overstriding is reaching your foot far out in front of your body, so it lands with your leg almost straight.
It's like pulling the handbrake a tiny bit on every step.
When runners take slightly quicker, shorter steps at the same speed, there's less braking and less load on the knee. [S10][S11]
Is it proven to prevent injuries or boost stamina? Not yet. The evidence is thin. [S11] But it's a small, low-risk tweak.
The fix: on an easy run, count the steps of one foot for 30 seconds, then multiply by 4. That's your steps per minute.
Add 5%. So 160 becomes 168. Use a metronome app or a playlist at that beat. [S10]
Run 3 to 5 one-minute chunks at the new rhythm, in easy runs only, not your longest run. Over 2 to 4 weeks, make the chunks longer. If your calves, Achilles or feet get sore, go back to your normal rhythm for a week.
Keep the same speed. Quicker, shorter steps, not faster running. [S10]
If your stride is pain-free, you don't have to change it. If your knee already hurts, see a physio first. [S11]
The mistake within the fix? Chasing a magic 180 steps a minute. Studies change cadence by 5 to 10% from the runner's own rhythm, and big jumps feel awkward. [S10][S11]

## Mistake 4: No recovery days
Training is the stress. Recovery is where you get fitter. [S12]
Pile on hard sessions without rest, and you stop improving. That can take weeks to months to recover from. [S12]
Think of a phone you never charge. The charging is where the battery gets bigger.
The fix: no back-to-back run days when you're starting. Couch to 5K puts a rest day between every run. [S13]
On other days, easy cycling or swimming keeps your engine ticking with less impact. [S20][S23]
And strength train at least 2 days a week. [S14] In a review of sports trials, people who did strength training ended up with about a third as many injuries. Stretching didn't help. Only a few trials tested strength work, and none were in runners. [S19]
Squats, calf raises, glute bridges and step-ups on a low, stable step. 2 to 3 sets of 8 to 15, stopping with a couple of reps left in the tank. Start with body weight, add weight slowly, and skip any exercise that hurts.
Need more rest? Watch for easy runs that feel unusually hard several times in a row, heavy legs, poor sleep, or frequent colds. If easing off for a week or two doesn't fix it, see a doctor.
The mistake within the fix? Turning your rest day into a hard leg session or a 5-a-side match. That isn't rest.

## Mistake 5: Running underfuelled
There are two ways to run on empty.
The first is running after hours without food. The NHS suggests a light snack 1 to 2 hours before, like a banana or toast. [S13]
For runs under an hour, you don't need gels or sports drinks. Water is enough. [S18]
On runs longer than about 60 to 90 minutes, start practising some carbohydrate during the run, working towards 30 to 60 grams an hour. [S18]
The second is eating too little, day after day, for the training you do. Doctors call it low energy availability. [S16][S17]
It can block your body's adaptation to training, so you stop getting fitter. It affects men and women, at any level. [S16][S17]
Think of a car on the reserve light. It still moves, but it switches off the extras, and the extras are the repairs that make you fitter.
After a run, eat a normal meal with plenty of carbohydrate. [S17]
See a doctor if you're losing weight without trying, if your periods stop or become irregular, or if you keep getting injured. [S17]
The mistake within the fix? Copying marathon fuelling on a 30-minute jog, or skipping meals while adding miles. [S17][S18]

## Stay safe
Before you start, talk to a doctor if you have a heart or lung condition or any health worries. [S13][S25]
If you get chest pain, faint or nearly faint, or feel unusually breathless, stop straight away. Don't push through it. [S25]
Call emergency services if chest pain doesn't go away when you stop, or spreads to your arm, back, neck or jaw. Otherwise, see a doctor before you run again. [S27]
Pain in one small spot on a bone that hurts when you press it, or pain at rest or at night, needs a doctor before you run again. [S20][S21]
Shin pain that builds as you run? Don't run through it. Swap to low-impact exercise and build back gradually. If it isn't getting better, see a doctor or physio. [S20]
In hot weather, run in the cool of the morning, go easier than usual, and drink cold fluids. [S13][S24]
If you feel dizzy or sick, stop running, get into the shade and cool down. Call emergency services if you're not better within 30 minutes, or straight away if someone becomes confused, or their skin is hot but they've stopped sweating. [S24]

## Your week
Here's a simple week. If you're on Couch to 5K, its runs go on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday. Monday: easy run-walk. Tuesday: strength. Wednesday: easy run with a few quick-feet minutes. [S13][S14]
Thursday: strength, or an easy swim or bike. Friday: rest. Saturday: your longest run, the same as last time or just a little longer. Sunday: rest. Play football or another sport? Count those sessions as run days. [S2][S13]
In one small lab study of men on exercise bikes, aerobic fitness was already up after 3 weeks. [S15] Couch to 5K is built to get beginners to 30 minutes of running in about 9 weeks, and it's fine if it takes longer. [S13]
So next time you set off, start slower than you think. Talk, don't gasp, and let the minutes add up.
