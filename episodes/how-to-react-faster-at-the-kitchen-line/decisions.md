# Editorial Decisions — How to react faster at the kitchen line

- **Target Audience:** Intermediate pickleball players (DUPR 3.0–4.5) struggling with fast-hands firefights, getting jammed at the non-volley zone (NVZ), or reacting too late to speed-ups.
- **Core Premise:** At 14 feet across the kitchen line, a 40 mph ball travels in ~240 ms — faster than the average human visual-motor reaction time (200–250 ms) plus paddle transit time (150–200 ms). Reacting to the ball after it leaves the paddle is physiologically too late. Speed at the kitchen line is not raw genetic reflexes; it is perceptual anticipation, quiet eye fixation, compact lever mechanics, and pre-impact split-stepping.
- **Tone:** Scientific, actionable, encouraging, athletic.
- **Visuals:** Mascot Coach Ember at kitchen line with pickleball paddle (`props: { r: "pickleball" }`), gaze reticles, ball trajectory vectors, reaction timeline breakdowns, kitchen firefight drills.
