## The five defensive traps
In padel, over 80 percent of points are won by the team controlling the net. [S1]

Defending is not about surviving indefinitely at the baseline. [S1]

Defense has one goal: neutralize the attack, evict opponents from the net, and take it back. [S2]

Most players fail because they bring habits from tennis, squash, or pickleball into an enclosed glass court. [S8]

Here are the 5 traps keeping you locked at the back. [S11]

## Mistake 1: Late corner turns
The hardest place to defend in padel is the 90-degree corner between the rear and side glass. [S8]

When a ball enters this corner, it hits both walls, creating a double glass rebound. [S14]

The fatal mistake is running straight into the corner facing the glass, waiting for the ball to hit both walls before turning. [S11]

By then, the ball bounces off the second wall directly into your hip, pinning you with zero room. [S14]

Trying to pivot on high-traction turf with your foot planted twists your knee, creating dangerous rotational shear on your meniscus. [S9][S10]

To fix this, read which glass the ball strikes first. [S14]

If the ball hits the side wall first, that is an opening double. [S14]

Open the door immediately: take a mini split-step, pivot your hips 180 degrees with flexed knees, and maintain 1 meter of clearance from the glass. [S10][S14]

Think of a revolving door: turn with the curve, never fight against it. [S14]

If the ball hits the back wall first, that is a closing double. [S14]

Do not spin in circles here. [S14]

Keep your chest facing the side wall, take two small steps forward, and catch the rebound as it leaves the back glass. [S14]

Train this with the Open-the-Door drill: start with gentle feeds for 4 sets of 10 side-first corner balls before adding match pace. [S14]

Pivot on the balls of your feet with soft knees before glass contact, guiding the rebound smoothly down the line. [S10][S14]

## Mistake 2: Hugging the back glass
When heavy smashes rain down from the net, your natural instinct is to back up until your heels touch the back glass. [S11]

This is called the safety illusion. [S11]

You think standing farther away gives you more time, but hugging the glass destroys your defense. [S5]

First, you have zero backswing room, so your paddle smacks the glass and you push weak balls forward. [S8][S10]

Second, tempered glass rebounds about 70 percent of incoming speed, launching deep shots 1 to 2 meters into the court. [S13]

If you are standing against the wall, the ball rebounds behind your body. [S11][S13]

Third, standing against the wall creates a massive 7-meter empty desert between you and the net, giving opponents easy drop shots. [S5][S8]

The fix is anchoring yourself in the defensive sweet spot: 0.8 meters behind the service line, and 1 meter in from the side glass. [S4][S5][S8]

Standing here is like a soccer goalkeeper positioning off the goal line. [S5]

When a short ball comes, step forward across the line. [S5]

When a deep ball comes, track backward with crossover steps, keeping a 1-meter deceleration buffer from the back wall. [S10]

Strike it at waist height as it rebounds forward into the court. [S10][S13][S14]

Train this with the Service-Line Tether drill: mark your sweet spot with cones 0.8 meters behind the service line. [S5]

Have a feeder alternate between a short ball and a deep glass ball for 4 sets of 8 cycles. [S4][S5]

Strike the short ball, and glide backward using controlled crossover steps to touch your cone. [S5][S10]

Track both ball and wall, letting the glass feed the rebound right into your strike zone. [S10][S14]

## Mistake 3: Blasting flat counter-punches
When players are pinned deep under heavy pressure, they panic and blast a flat, 60-mile-per-hour missile straight at net players. [S3][S11]

This is the single most self-destructive shot in padel. [S3]

At 7 meters away, net players hold solid composite rackets with no strings. [S8][S9]

A hard flat shot hits their racket and rebounds with devastating pace, giving them an instant reflex winner. [S7][S13]

Attempting flat speed-ups from deep court increases your unforced errors by over 4-fold. [S3]

Firing hard at net players is like throwing rocks at a tank: you only give them the ammunition to crush you. [S7]

The true weapon of padel defense is the high reset lob. [S2]

In professional padel, players choose the lob in over 60 percent of defensive transitions, keeping the rally alive and neutralizing the attack nearly two-thirds of the time. [S2]

A proper defensive lob arches 5 to 8 meters into the air, creating 3.5 to 4.5 seconds of flight hang time. [S6]

Those 4 seconds break opponents' rhythm, force both volleyers to sprint 7 meters backward, and give you time to capture the net. [S2][S6]

To hit it, bend your knees, open your paddle face 25 degrees, and lift smoothly from your legs without snapping your wrist. [S9][S10][S14]

Practice the Stopwatch Lob drill: hit lobs from the baseline with a partner timing the ball in the air. [S6]

Do 4 sets of 10 repetitions, counting only lobs that hang for at least 3.5 seconds and land in the back third. [S6]

## Mistake 4: Forgetting the chiquita
Sometimes opponents anticipate your lob, standing 3 to 4 meters back from the net with overheads cocked. [S7][S14]

Or you play in an indoor club with a low ceiling where sky-high lobs are restricted. [S6]

When amateurs cannot lob, they usually hit a medium-pace drive right at the opponent's chest. [S7][S11]

This lets net players lean forward and punch an aggressive angle off the side fence. [S7][S14]

The secret weapon here is the chiquita, which means "the little one." [S7][S14]

A chiquita is a slow, dipping slice that crosses inches over the net and dies right at your opponent's shoelaces. [S7][S14]

Padel net height is 0.88 meters at the center. [S8]

If opponents contact the ball above the net, they can spike downward into the glass. [S7][S8]

When your chiquita dips below net height, physics forces them to volley upward just to clear the tape. [S7][S8]

The instant they hit that floating upward ball, your defense flips into an attack. [S7]

To execute it, cushion the ball with a compact slice backswing, applying gentle backspin so it dips immediately. [S10][S14]

Most importantly, only advance once the ball visibly dips below net tape height. [S7]

Take two quick steps forward into the transition zone, split-step with paddle chest-high, and pounce on their floating volley. [S4][S7][S14]

Train this with the Shoelace Drop drill: place target hoops 1.5 meters behind the net. [S7]

Feed balls from the baseline, alternating between high reset lobs and soft dipping chiquitas, aiming for 4 sets of 12 reps. [S7][S14]

## Mistake 5: The center ball freeze
The padel court is 10 meters wide, and opponents love driving the ball straight down the center seam between partners. [S4][S8]

Amateur pairs either both freeze, letting the ball roll off the glass, or both swing, clashing rackets and risking hand injuries. [S4][S9][S11]

Even worse, when one player gets pulled wide into a corner, the other stands still, leaving a 4-meter canyon down the middle. [S4]

Spatial tracking shows that elite pairs move as a synchronized unit, connected by an invisible 3-meter string. [S4]

When your partner slides wide to the right corner, you must slide right to seal the center seam. [S4]

Next, establish middle priority before the point begins. [S4][S14]

The default rule is forehand in the middle: for two right-handed players, the left-side player takes contested center balls because their forehand has natural leverage. [S4][S14]

If you are playing cross-court, the player diagonally opposite the hitter covers the drive. [S4][S14]

Above all, enforce the early call-or-yield rule: the moment the ball crosses the net tape, shout "Mine." [S11][S14]

If no call is heard, the secondary partner must immediately yield and clear the hitting corridor. [S4][S14]

Calling early eliminates hesitation and lets your partner step aside to cover the next angle. [S4][S14]

Practice the Center Calling drill: have a feeder fire rapid groundstrokes down the center line for 4 sets of 12 balls. [S4][S14]

Call "Mine" while the ball is airborne over the net, scoop a defensive lob, and coordinate seamless coverage without a single collision. [S4][S14]

## The complete defensive blueprint
To lock these 5 fixes into your muscle memory, follow this simple weekly court plan. [S14]

In session one, spend 15 minutes on corner glass reads: 4 sets of 10 reps of the Open-the-Door drill, alternating side-first and back-first rebounds. [S14]

In session two, dedicate 15 minutes to transition mastery: 4 sets of 10 Stopwatch Lobs and 4 sets of 8 Chiquita-and-Pounce sequences. [S6][S7]

During match play, make the sweet spot your home base: 0.8 meters behind the service line, connected to your partner by that invisible string. [S4][S5]

Always complete an 8-minute warm-up with dynamic ankle calf lunges, lateral hip openers, and standing thoracic spine rotations with your racket across your shoulders. [S9][S10][S12]

If you feel sudden knee locking, sharp wrist shock after contact, or cannot bear weight on your ankle, stop immediately and see a sports physiotherapist or sports medicine physician. [S9][S10][S12][S15]
