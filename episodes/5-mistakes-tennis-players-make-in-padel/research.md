# Research brief: 5 mistakes tennis players make in padel

Audience: amateur and club athletes, 16–40. Many are intermediate-to-advanced tennis players making the transition to padel, carrying strong racket skills but deeply ingrained tennis biomechanics and baseline tactical habits that actively backfire in an enclosed 10×20 m glass court.
Every factual line carries [S#], which maps directly to `sources.json`.
Lines marked **(coach-derived)** indicate practical drills, rules of thumb, and training progressions built from sports science principles, biomechanical analysis, and elite coaching practices.

## Promise
Unlearn the 5 ingrained tennis habits that sabotage your padel game, master the physics of the glass, and dominate the net with padel-specific mechanics.

### Why this order:
- **Mistake 1 (The Power Trap)** hits the immediate ego barrier of every tennis player: the instinct to hit hard and finish points with raw power, which instantly fails against glass.
- **Mistake 2 (The Lob)** introduces the counter-intuitive tactical core of padel: the soft, high ball is your deadliest offensive weapon.
- **Mistake 3 (Baseline Camping)** zooms out to the macro-strategy and court geography: moving from tennis baseline comfort to padel net dominance.
- **Mistake 4 (Wall Panic)** tackles the specific technical terror of the back glass and rebound angles.
- **Mistake 5 (Big Swings & Overheads)** breaks down the specific biomechanical strokes (punch volley, bandeja, víbora) and directly protects the player's elbow and shoulder from chronic injury.

## Mistake 1: The Power Trap (Over-Hitting & Flat Smashes)

### What it is / biomechanics & physics
In tennis, hitting the ball hard and deep into the open court produces winners or forces errors because the boundary ends at the baseline. In padel, the court is strictly bounded by tempered glass walls and metallic mesh on an enclosed 10×20 m rectangle [S14]. 
- **The Glass Rebound Law:** Tempered padel glass (10–12 mm thick) provides a high coefficient of restitution ($e \approx 0.65\text{--}0.70$), returning 65% to 70% of incoming ball velocity after the bounce [S7].
- When a tennis player smashes flat or blasts a full-power forehand drive from deep, the ball hits the floor, slams into the back glass, and rebounds with huge velocity straight into the middle of the court, hanging at waist height [S7][S15].
- The harder you hit it into the glass, the easier and more offensive the ball becomes for the defending opponent, who simply stands balanced and crushes an overhead or passing shot [S2][S7][S15].
- **The Spin Paradox:** In tennis, heavy topspin creates dipping trajectories that push opponents back. In padel, when heavy topspin hits the vertical glass wall, surface friction causes the ball to "climb" the glass and kick upward into a high, floating sitter [S7]. In contrast, slice/underspin skids along the turf and bites downward off the glass, dying before the opponent can lift it [S2][S15].
- **Shot Selection at the Elite Level:** In World Padel Tour analysis, flat/power smashes account for only ~12% of attacking shots, whereas volleys represent ~65% and controlled slice overheads (*bandejas*) represent ~23% [S2]. Smashes are reserved strictly for short balls inside 2–3 meters of the net [S2][S15].

### Analogy
Hitting a hard tennis drive into a padel glass wall is like throwing a rubber ball as hard as you can against a brick fireplace. It doesn't get past anyone; it ricochets straight back into your own face with full interest.

### Sport moments
- **The Tennis Trap:** A former college tennis player receives a floating ball at the baseline, sets up with a massive open-stance windshield-wiper forehand, and absolutely detonates the ball. The ball flies past the opponents at the net, smacks the back glass, and rebounds 4 meters into the court, where the opposing defender calmly taps a drop volley into the fence for a winner while the tennis player watches in disbelief.
- **The Failed Smash:** Jumping from the service line to hit a 100 mph flat tennis overhead, only for the ball to bounce on the opponent's side, hammer the back glass, fly back over the net, and leave the hitter completely out of position while the opponent counters into an empty court [S7][S15].

### The Fix: Controlled Depth & The Low-Rebound Rule
- **Rule of Thumb (coach-derived):** "If you can't hit it out of the court (*por tres* or *por cuatro*), don't smash flat." Keep pace at 60–70% speed, aiming low at the opponent's feet (*chiquita*) or with slice into the back corners [S2][S15].
- **Drill 1: The Glass Rebound Gauge Drill (coach-derived):**
  - **Setup:** Feeder feeds baseline balls. Player hits groundstrokes with varying pace.
  - **Target:** The rebound off the back glass must bounce a second time BEFORE the service line (6.95 m from the net) [S14].
  - **Dosage:** 4 sets × 10 reps each side (forehand and backhand). 60 s rest between sets. Focus on applying backspin/slice rather than topspin.
- **Drill 2: The Two-Pace Transition Drill (coach-derived):**
  - Hit a soft *chiquita* dipping into the feet of the volleyer, then step forward to read the weak pop-up volley. 3 sets × 8 rallies.

### Common mistake within the fix
Over-correcting by tapping the ball softly with no spin, creating floaters that sit up before reaching the glass. The goal is not zero pace, but applying heavy underspin/slice so the ball skids low upon glass impact [S2][S15].

## Mistake 2: The Lob Misunderstanding

### What it is / tactical science
In tennis, the lob is typically viewed as a desperate defensive hail mary when stretched miles wide, or a high-risk topspin trick shot over an aggressive net-rusher. In padel, sports science demonstrates that **the lob is the single most important offensive and positional stroke in the entire sport** [S3][S4][S15].
- **Territorial Reset:** In competitive match analysis, baseline players choose the lob in **60.6% of successful positional exchanges** to regain the net, compared to just 39.4% for groundstrokes and passing shots [S3].
- **Why Passing Shots Fail in Padel:** A padel court is only 10 meters wide, manned by two players at the net holding solid rackets [S14]. Trying to hit low passing shots down the line or cross-court gives the net pair easy reflex volleys; even if the ball gets past them, it rebounds off the back glass, keeping the rally alive [S2][S7].
- **Flight Time as a Weapon:** A high defensive/offensive lob (traveling 6 to 8 meters into the air) stays in flight for 2.5 to 3.5 seconds [S3][S7]. This provides both baseline partners sufficient time to sprint forward together in tandem, securing the net position before the ball lands [S3][S6].
- **Target Depth:** An optimal padel lob bounces between the opponent's service line and the back glass, forcing the opponents to retreat, turn their shoulders, and play a difficult back-pedaling *bandeja* or let it hit the back glass [S3][S15].

### Analogy
In chess, a padel lob is not throwing your hands in the air; it is playing an aggressive castle or pawn push that evicts your opponent's queen from the center of the board. It forces them to surrender the high ground.

### Sport moments
- A tennis player stuck at the baseline tries three consecutive flat drives down the line into two waiting net players, who comfortably block every ball until the tennis player tires out or nets the ball.
- Contrast: A seasoned padel player bends their knees, opens the racket face, lofts a smooth, high ball 7 meters in the air into the left corner. The net opponents are forced to sprint backward; the lobbing pair comfortably walks up to the net and puts away the ensuing weak reply [S3][S15].

### The Fix: The High-Arc Baseline Eviction
- **Biomechanics of the Padel Lob:** Do not flick the wrist. Bend the knees to get under the ball's center of mass, open the racket face (15°–25° upward tilt), and use smooth knee extension and a controlled low-to-high shoulder swing with a firm continental grip [S10][S15].
- **Drill 1: Lob Target Box Drill (coach-derived, based on [S3][S10][S15]):**
  - **Setup:** Mark a 2×2 meter target box in the back corners between the service line and back glass.
  - **Execution:** Feeder hits medium-paced volleys from the net. Baseline player executes deep, high lobs aiming into the target box.
  - **Dosage:** 4 sets × 12 reps (6 forehand, 6 backhand). Rest 45 s between sets. Target: minimum 8 out of 12 landing inside the deep zone with sufficient height (>5 meters apex).
- **Drill 2: "Lob & Charge" Transition Simulation (coach-derived, based on [S3][S6]):**
  - Baseline pair lobs deep. The moment the ball clears the net players' reach, both players sprint forward to the net line (2–3 m from the net). Play the point out live. 5 sets of 4 points.

### Common mistake within the fix
Hitting the lob too shallow (landing near the service line). A short lob in padel is an immediate death sentence, offering the net player a free overhead smash or aggressive víbora directly into the corners [S2][S15].

## Mistake 3: Baseline Camping

### What it is / match dynamics
Tennis is an overwhelmingly baseline-dominated sport in the modern era. On the ATP and WTA tours, **over 75% to 80% of points are contested entirely from the baseline**, with net approaches occurring on only 15% to 20% of points [S16]. As a result, tennis players develop an instinctive comfort zone standing 1 to 2 meters behind the baseline.
- **The Net Dominance Law of Padel:** In padel, staying at the baseline is a statistical death sentence. Sports science research reveals that **over 80% (80% to 83%) of all points won in professional padel are won by the pair occupying the net zone** [S1][S8].
- Winning pairs perform at least one attacking action (volley, bandeja, smash) in **85% of points**, while losing pairs perform zero attacking actions in 47% of points [S2]. Winning pairs score ~34% more points at the net and make significantly fewer unforced errors from that position [S1].
- **Court Geometry and Time:** The distance from the net to the back wall is only 10 meters [S14]. If you remain at the baseline, you concede 70% of the playable court geometry to your opponents, giving them sharp angles into the side wire fence and wide glass [S1][S5][S14].
- **Tandem Movement Failure:** Tennis players are used to singles play, moving independently. In padel, partners must move synchronized like two foosball figures attached to the same metal rod ("two up, two back") [S6][S15]. When one tennis player rushes the net alone while their partner camps at the back, they create a massive diagonal channel that opponents exploit with ease [S5][S6].

### Analogy
Camping at the baseline in padel is like playing soccer with your goalkeeper and all ten outfield players permanently packed inside the 6-yard box. You might block a few shots, but you can never score, and eventually the siege breaks you.

### Sport moments
- A tennis player hits a beautiful deep groundstroke, admires the shot from behind the baseline, and watches in shock as the opposing team softly drops the ball short into the side fence for an unreturnable winner.
- The "split pair" nightmare: Player A runs forward to retrieve a drop shot, but Player B stays anchored on the baseline. The opposing team chips a simple volley straight into the wide-open gap between them [S5][S6].

### The Fix: The 3-Meter Net Lock & Tandem Cord
- **The Net Position Zone:** Position yourself approximately 2.5 to 3.0 meters from the net (roughly two giant strides behind the net cord), never touching the net (which loses the point under Rule 13a) [S1][S14]. This distance allows you to intercept volleys above net height while remaining capable of retreating for lobs [S1][S15].
- **Drill 1: Tandem Movement (The Invisible Rope) (coach-derived, based on [S6][S15]):**
  - **Setup:** Two players hold a 3-meter elastic resistance band or cord between their non-dominant hands.
  - **Execution:** Rally back and forth. When Player A moves forward, Player B MUST match their forward depth. When Player A retreats for a lob, both retreat in unison.
  - **Dosage:** 3 sets × 5 minutes of continuous continuous movement rallying. Rest 90 s between sets.
- **Drill 2: Serve-and-Volley Sprint (The Australian/Conventional Rush) (coach-derived, based on [S6]):**
  - Server serves below waist height [S14], immediately drives off the back foot, and takes 3 explosive forward strides to establish position at the net line before the returner hits the ball.
  - **Dosage:** 20 serves per side (40 total) focusing purely on first-step acceleration and immediate split-step at the net line [S6].

### Common mistake within the fix
Creeping forward too close to the net (less than 1 meter). Getting within arm's reach of the net makes you an effortless target for any medium lob over your head and restricts your reaction time to reflex volleys [S1][S14].

## Mistake 4: Wall Panic & Glass Misjudgment

### What it is / physics & rebound kinematics
In tennis, if a ball gets past your body, the point is over. This builds an intense reflexive panic in transitioning tennis players: the moment a ball travels deep toward the back corners, their adrenaline spikes, they scramble backward, and they attempt frantic, contorted half-volleys off their shoelaces before the ball reaches the glass.
- **The Glass as a Decelerator:** The glass wall is not your enemy; it is your ultimate ally. When a fast, heavy shot travels past you, letting it bounce on the artificial turf and strike the back glass absorbs linear momentum and causes the ball to project **forward**, right back into your strike zone at a slower, manageable speed [S7][S14].
- **Law of Reflection ($\theta_i = \theta_r$):** On smooth tempered glass, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection [S7]. A ball coming straight into the back wall bounces straight out; a ball entering at a 30° angle exits at a 30° angle toward the side fence [S7].
- **The "Turning the Door" Mechanical Breakdown:** Tennis players track the ball with their eyes facing the net, backpedaling straight back. When the ball hits the glass and shoots forward, it traps them directly against the wall, leaving no room to swing [S14][S15].
- **Padel Mechanical Reality:** To play off the glass, you must "open the door"—the instant the ball crosses the service line toward the glass, rotate your shoulders 90° sideways, turn your hips, let the ball pass your belly button, track it with your body, and strike it on its forward path away from the wall [S14][S15].

### Analogy
Rushing to hit a ball before it hits the back glass is like trying to jump through a closing revolving door at full speed. Step aside, let the door rotate past you, and walk through smoothly on the other side.

### Sport moments
- A tennis player hears the ball hit the court near the back line, lunges backward in panic, swipes with a wristy backhand, shanks the frame into the fence, and falls backward onto the turf—when simply taking two steps forward and waiting would have delivered a floaty, waist-high ball right onto their sweet spot [S7][S15].
- Misreading the "double glass" (corner rebound): The ball strikes the back glass, then clips the side glass, completely spinning away from the player who turned the wrong way [S7].

### The Fix: "Open the Door" & Wait for the Rebound
- **Technical Cue:** Turn shoulders early, racket set at hip height, let the ball pass your body, wait for the bounce off the glass, and hit the ball out in front as it moves back toward the net [S14][S15].
- **Drill 1: The "Catch and Hit" Glass Confidence Drill (coach-derived, based on [S7][S15]):**
  - **Setup:** Feeder stands at the net and feeds medium-to-fast balls deep into the back corner.
  - **Phase 1 (No Racket):** Player stands in ready position, turns sideways as the ball approaches, lets the ball bounce on the turf, hit the back glass, and catches the ball with their dominant hand out in front at waist height. (2 sets × 10 catches each side).
  - **Phase 2 (With Racket):** Same movement, now driving the ball with a compact slice back across the net.
  - **Dosage:** 4 sets × 12 reps (6 forehand wall exits, 6 backhand wall exits). Rest 60 s between sets.
- **Drill 2: Corner Rebound Tracking (Double Glass) (coach-derived, based on [S7]):**
  - Feeder hits angled balls into the back-to-side glass corner. Player reads the primary wall (back wall first vs side wall first) and moves in a small circular semi-circle to strike the outgoing ball. 3 sets × 8 reps per corner.

### Common mistake within the fix
Standing completely still while waiting for the ball to rebound. If you stand flat-footed, the ball will hit the glass, rebound directly into your chest or legs, and cramp your swing. You must take dynamic adjustment steps forward and away from the rebound path [S7][S15].

## Mistake 5: Big Tennis Swings & Overheads (No Bandeja/Víbora)

### What it is / biomechanics & injury epidemiology
This is the single most dangerous mistake for a tennis player transitioning to padel—both for their tactical success and their long-term orthopedic health.
- **The Large Backswing Handicap:** Tennis players use sweeping, looped backswings with semi-Western or Western grips to generate heavy topspin from the baseline. On a 10×20 m padel court, where the average point lasts only 10–15 seconds and features rapid 8–10 shot exchanges at close quarters [S9], a big backswing means the racket head is still traveling backward when the ball arrives [S10].
- **Contact Point Behind the Body:** The player is forced to hit the ball late, behind the frontal plane of their body, jamming their arm and flicking the wrist to compensate [S10][S12].
- **The Injury Mechanism (Padel Elbow / Lateral Epicondylitis):**
  - Padel rackets are solid, non-stringed composite frames (350–375 g) made of carbon fiber and an EVA foam core [S10][S13][S14]. They lack the elastic, vibration-absorbing string bed of a tennis racket [S10].
  - When a player strikes a 56–59.4 g padel ball late or off-center with an excessive wrist snap, high-frequency impact vibrations and torsional stress are transmitted directly into the forearm extensors, specifically the **Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB)** at the lateral epicondyle [S10][S12][S14].
  - Lateral epicondylitis accounts for **over 20% of all padel injuries**, representing the #1 upper-limb medical complaint in the sport [S11][S12].
- **Overhead Mechanics (Shoulder Impingement):**
  - In tennis, the overhead smash and serve rely on full vertical arm extension, severe lumbar hyperextension, and violent internal shoulder rotation with downward forearm pronation [S10][S15].
  - In padel, hitting overheads this way causes two massive failures:
    1. **Tactical failure:** As shown in Mistake 1, flat smashes bounce off the back glass and set up opponents [S2][S7].
    2. **Orthopedic failure:** Forcing rapid internal rotation and deceleration with a stiff, heavy padel racket pinches the supraspinatus tendon against the acromion, causing **subacromial shoulder impingement and rotator cuff tendinopathy** (which accounts for ~14.8% of padel injuries) [S11][S13].
- **The Padel Solution: Bandeja and Víbora:**
  - **The Bandeja (The Defensive Tray):** Struck with a continental grip at eye/ear height out to the side (not fully extended overhead) [S15]. The elbow remains flexed at 120°–140°. The racket face is open, cutting underneath the ball with slice, and finishes across the neck like a scarf [S15]. It generates zero dangerous shoulder internal rotation, keeps the ball low off the back glass, and allows the player to retain the net [S2][S15].
  - **The Víbora (The Aggressive Viper):** Struck at head height with side-spin, brushing around the outside of the ball to cause violent horizontal skidding off the glass and side fence [S15].
  - **The Padel Volley:** Compact punch volley. The racket head never travels behind the lead shoulder line; power comes from forward weight transfer onto the front foot and a firm continental wrist [S10][S15].

### Analogy
Playing padel with a full tennis stroke is like trying to drive a 16-wheel semi-truck around a tight indoor go-kart track. The engine has plenty of power, but the turning radius is far too wide, and you will smash into every wall.

### Sport moments
- A tennis player at the net sees a floating ball, winds up with a massive 3-foot looping backswing, contacts the ball 6 inches behind their hip, shanks it into the side mesh, and immediately clutches their outer elbow in pain [S10][S14].
- A tennis player jumps vertically for a high lob, snaps their wrist downward with a 100% tennis serve motion, jars their shoulder, and watches the ball bounce waist-high off the back glass into the opponent's counter-attack [S7][S10][S15].

### The Fix: The Compact Punch & The Bandeja Platform
- **Volley Rule:** Continental grip only [S15]. Racket head kept strictly in front of the eyes in the ready position. The backswing stops at the shoulder line—rotate the chest, step forward with the opposite foot, and punch through with downward slice [S10][S15].
- **Drill 1: The Wall Behind the Back Volley Drill (coach-derived, based on [S10][S15]):**
  - **Setup:** Stand with your back 20 cm away from the back glass wall.
  - **Execution:** Feeder feeds volleys from across the net. Volley the ball cleanly. If your racket touches the glass on the backswing, the rep doesn't count.
  - **Dosage:** 4 sets × 15 volleys (alternate forehand and backhand). Rest 45 s. Forces an immediate, compact unit turn without backswing loop [S10][S15].
- **Drill 2: Bandeja Scarf Progression (coach-derived, based on [S15]):**
  - **Step 1:** Shadow swing: set racket up like a tray (elbow bent at 90°–120° at ear level, non-dominant hand pointing at the sky).
  - **Step 2:** Slide racket sideways through impact at ear height, cutting under the ball with slice, finishing with the racket wrapping around the opposite collarbone ("put on the scarf") [S15].
  - **Dosage:** 3 sets × 12 fed overheads from the service line. Aim cross-court into the back corner glass.

### Common mistake within the fix
Letting the wrist collapse or "snap" at contact on the volley or bandeja. In padel, a loose wrist leads to mishits on the solid racket face and strains the ECRB tendon. Keep the wrist firm and locked in slight extension throughout impact [S10][S12][S15].

## Myths & Common Mistakes

### Myth 1: "Padel is just mini-tennis with walls."
- **Evidence against:** Padel is fundamentally an inverted tactical sport [S1][S2][S7]. In tennis, hitting hard into open space ends the point. In padel, hitting hard against the perimeter wall feeds the opponent [S7]. In tennis, baseline is safety; in padel, baseline is a trap [S1][S8]. Racket physics (solid perforated plate vs string bed) and ball dynamics completely alter the kinetic chain and stroke mechanics [S10][S14].

### Myth 2: "The lob is a weak, defensive shot."
- **Evidence against:** Match analysis proves the lob is the most potent offensive transition shot in padel, utilized in >60% of successful net recoveries [S3][S4]. A deep lob strips the opposing pair of their dominant net position (>80% win rate zone) and hands territorial dominance to the lobbers [S1][S3].

### Myth 3: "You should smash every high ball as hard as you can."
- **Evidence against:** WPT finals data shows flat smashes represent only ~12% of attacking overheads [S2]. Smashes hit from behind the service line fail to clear the 3-meter glass wall and rebound straight into the court, granting the defense an easy counter-attack [S7][S15]. Elite players rely on the *bandeja* (~23%) to place the ball deep with slice, keeping the ball low and retaining the net [S2][S15].

### Myth 4: "Heavy topspin is the best way to control the ball."
- **Evidence against:** Topspin grips the vertical glass and kicks violently upward at waist-to-chest height, setting up defenders for easy put-aways [S7]. Slice (underspin) creates backspin that causes the ball to bite downward upon hitting the glass and skid along the ground, making counter-attack virtually impossible [S2][S7][S15].

### Myth 5: "Tennis players don't need to change their grips."
- **Evidence against:** Semi-Western and Western forehand grips force excessive wrist manipulation on low wall bounces and make punch volleys unstable, transmitting extreme shock to the forearm extensors [S10][S15]. Padel requires the **Continental ("hammer") grip** for roughly 80% of all strokes, providing stability, compact leverage, and effortless transition between forehand and backhand [S10][S15].

## Safety & Injury Prevention

### 1. Lateral Epicondylitis ("Padel Elbow")
- **Mechanism:** Repeated eccentric contraction of the Extensor Carpi Radialis Brevis (ECRB) during off-center hits, late contact points behind the body, and excessive wrist flicking on a non-stringed composite racket [S10][S12].
- **Prevention Protocols (Bernal, 2023 [S12]; Demeco et al., 2022 [S10]):**
  - **Equipment selection:** Transitioning tennis players should start with a **round-shaped racket** (low balance / center of gravity close to the handle) and a **soft EVA or FOAM core**, which absorbs impact vibrations far better than stiff diamond-shaped carbon rackets [S10][S12][S13].
  - **Grip sizing:** Add 1 to 2 overgrips so the fingers do not overlap completely; an undersized grip forces excessive clenching of the forearm flexors and extensors [S12].
  - **Eccentric conditioning (coach-derived, based on [S10][S12]):** Tyler Twist with a flexible resistance bar (3 sets × 15 reps daily) and eccentric wrist extensions with a 1–2 kg dumbbell (3 sets × 12 reps, 3 s lowering phase).

### 2. Rotator Cuff & Subacromial Impingement ("Padel Shoulder")
- **Mechanism:** Violent humeral internal rotation and rapid arm deceleration during tennis-style overhead smashes, pinching the supraspinatus tendon against the acromion [S10][S11][S13].
- **Prevention Protocols:**
  - Transition from the flat overhead smash to the **bandeja**, which maintains elbow flexion (120°–140°) and keeps the contact point lower and more laterally positioned, eliminating aggressive impingement angles [S12][S15].
  - **Scapular & Rotator Cuff Strengthening (coach-derived, based on [S12]):** Banded external rotations at 0° and 90° abduction (3 sets × 15 reps, 3×/week) and prone Y-T-W raises for lower trapezius and serratus anterior activation.

### 3. Red Flags — When to Stop and See a Professional
- **Elbow:** Constant burning pain over the lateral epicondyle radiating down the forearm, pain at rest, or inability to hold a coffee mug or turn a doorknob (indicates acute-on-chronic ECRB tendinopathy or tear) [S10][S12].
- **Shoulder:** Inability to raise the arm overhead without severe sharp catching, nocturnal pain that disrupts sleep when lying on the shoulder, or sudden weakness during overhead motions (indicates possible rotator cuff tear or severe subacromial bursitis) [S11][S12].
- **Lower Limb / Achilles:** Sudden popping sensation or feeling like someone kicked the back of the calf/heel during explosive push-offs (Rule out Achilles tendon rupture or "tennis leg" / medial gastrocnemius tear) [S11][S12].

## Unverified / Anecdotal Claims (Do NOT present as scientific fact)

1. *"A tennis player can become a 4.0/category 2 padel player in under 3 months."*
   - **Status:** Unverified anecdotal coaching claim. While tennis players possess superior hand-eye coordination and volley reflexes, their ingrained muscle memory (big backswings, wall panic, flat smashes) often creates severe tactical bottlenecks that take 6–12 months of structured coaching to unlearn.
2. *"Carbon 18K rackets are always better for ex-tennis players because they hit harder."*
   - **Status:** False and dangerous equipment myth. 18K carbon creates an extremely stiff surface. When paired with a hard EVA core, it transmits massive shock waves to the elbow, accelerating lateral epicondylitis in players who do not yet hit the sweet spot consistently [S10][S12].
3. *"Never hit topspin in padel under any circumstances."*
   - **Status:** Tactical exaggeration. While topspin off the back glass sits up, topspin is used deliberately in specific advanced attacking shots like the *rulo* (topspin smash diagonally toward the side metallic mesh) and *por tres* kick smashes to make the ball bounce over the 3-meter side wall [S14][S15]. However, for novice-to-intermediate transitioning tennis players, adopting slice as the default is the essential first step.
