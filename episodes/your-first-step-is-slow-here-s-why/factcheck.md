## Safety
| # | Advice | Risk | Required change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Falling start cue: *"Stand tall on your toes. Without bending at the waist, fall forward like a tree until you're about to hit the ground, then punch one knee forward into a 10-meter sprint."* | **Fall / Face-plant & Ankle Instability Risk:** Standing high on tiptoes reduces balance stability and pre-plantarflexes the ankle (contradicting the dorsiflexion pre-tension cue). Waiting until "about to hit the ground" exceeds the safe tipping point for amateur athletes who may hesitate or freeze, risking severe facial or joint impact. | Modify cue to initiate from flat feet and break at the tipping point: *"Stand tall with feet hip-width. Without bending at the waist, fall forward from the ankles. The moment your balance tips past your toes, punch one knee forward into a 10-meter sprint."* |
| 2 | Heavy sled push prescription: *"If you have a weighted sled, push it for 10 to 15 meters. Heavy sled work directly develops horizontal force production. [S9][S10] Do 4 falling starts and 3 sled pushes, resting 2 minutes between reps."* | **Lumbar Spine Collapse & Adductor Overload:** Prescribing an unquantified "heavy" sled push without load guidelines or form boundaries can lead amateurs to load excessive weight, breaking posture into spinal flexion or straining adductor muscles during grinding steps. | Add load and form boundary: *"If you have a weighted sled, push it for 10 to 15 meters using a moderate-to-heavy load where you can maintain a rigid neutral spine and continuous forward drive without stumbling (or substitute with heavy resistance band starts)."* |
