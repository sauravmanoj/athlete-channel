# The levels of a pickleball player
Promise: find out which of the five pickleball levels you're really at — from learning the rules to having no weak spots — and the one skill that moves you up to the next level.
Framework: five levels — Starter, Rallier, Dropper, Resetter, No Weak Spots — joined by one thread: miss less.

## Hook
You smash the ball as hard as you can. It comes straight back at your feet, and you lose the point. Again.
To climb the pickleball ladder, hitting harder isn't the main thing. Missing less is. [S1][S3][S7]
Today: the five levels, which one you're really at, and the skill that moves you up.

## The five levels
USA Pickleball's skill levels run from 1.0 up to 5.5-plus, mostly in half-point steps. [S1][S2]
Its own overview groups them into five tiers. [S2] My five levels are cut slightly differently, one skill each, and the names are mine.
Level 1, the Starter. Level 2, the Rallier. Level 3, the Dropper. Level 4, the Resetter. And Level 5, No Weak Spots.
And there's a hidden thread. At 3.0, the official checklist asks you to land serves, returns and volleys 7 times out of 10. At 3.5, it's 8. At 4.0, it's 9. At 4.5 and 5.0, it's 10 out of 10. [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7]
So: new skills at every level, and fewer misses on all of them.
A DUPR rating is different. It comes from your match results, not a skill checklist. [S2][S9] When I quote DUPR numbers, they only cover players with an active doubles rating, not everyone who plays. [S11]

## Level 1: The Starter
The official 2.5 player can keep a short rally going and has a basic ability to keep score. [S1]
It's like learning to drive in an empty car park. Rules of the road first. Speed later.
Rule one is the two-bounce rule. The serve must bounce, then the return must bounce. Only after that can anyone volley. [S8]
Rule two is the kitchen: the zone 7 feet from the net on each side. You can't volley while standing in it. [S8]
The classic Starter moment: you serve, rush the net, volley the return, and lose the point. [S8]
Among players with a DUPR rating, about 31% are below 3.0. [S11]
Before any drill in this video, warm up for 5 to 8 minutes: light jogging, side shuffles, leg swings, walking lunges and easy calf raises. Then soft dinks, building up to drives. Doctors who study pickleball injuries recommend a proper warm-up. [S15]
To move up, get the ball in play. Drill 1: the 10-ball serve test. Serve 10 from each side and count how many land in. 3 rounds, 2 or 3 times a week. Your target is 7 out of 10. [S3]
Drill 2: the dink count. With a partner at the kitchen line, trade soft shots that bounce in the kitchen. 3 rounds of 2 minutes. Aim for 10 in a row, then 20. [S3][S8]
New to sport, or coming back after a break? Build your court time up gradually, and don't play through pain.
The mistake? Swinging hard to win points. At this level, the game is keeping the ball in. [S1][S3]

## Level 2: The Rallier
Level 2 is the 3.0 player: medium-paced forehands, a basic dink rally, and a backhand they avoid. [S1]
Among DUPR-rated players, 3.0 to 3.49 is the single biggest rating band: about 29%. [S11]
The Rallier still makes frequent unforced errors. Those are misses nobody forced you into. [S1]
And here's the trap. The Rallier hits a medium-paced third shot from the back with no real plan. The opponents are already at the net, and they volley it back at your feet. [S1][S8]
Think of playing catch. Your job is to keep the ball going, not to win the throw.
To move up, return deep and get forward. The 3.5 checklist asks you to place serves and returns deep, land them 8 times out of 10, and move up to the kitchen line when you get the chance. [S4]
The drill: return and run. Your partner serves. You return deep, then move up to the kitchen line before the ball comes back. 3 sets of 10. [S4]
Move forward under control. Don't sprint and lunge. In one clinic's records, running or lunging forward was the most common way players hurt a foot or ankle. [S17]
The mistake? Hiding from your backhand. Train it now, not later. [S1]

## Level 3: The Dropper
Level 3, the 3.5 player, is building the most famous shot in pickleball: the third-shot drop. [S1]
Why that shot? Because of the two-bounce rule, the serving team hits the third shot from the back, while the returners can already be at the net. [S8]
A drop is a soft shot that floats over the net and lands in the kitchen. It's hard for the players at the net to attack, so it buys you time to move forward. [S1][S24]
Think of a parachute: while the ball floats down, you walk in. A hard drive is a rocket. Fast, but it can come right back at you.
About 24% of DUPR players are rated 3.5 to 3.99. [S11]
Drill 1: the drop ladder. Start at the kitchen line with a dink rally. Then step back 2 steps and keep landing soft shots in the kitchen. When 4 land, step back again, all the way to the baseline. [S24]
Drill 2: third shot must be a drop. Play a game to 11 where every third shot has to be a drop, even if it loses you the point. [S24]
The mistake? Hitting a beautiful drop and admiring it from the baseline. The drop exists to get you to the net. [S1]

## Level 4: The Resetter
Level 4 covers 4.0 and 4.5. These players are learning the reset. [S1]
A reset is a soft block. It takes the pace off a hard ball and drops it into the kitchen. The rally goes from frantic back to calm. [S1][S6]
Picture catching an egg. Soft hands that give with the ball.
Why does this matter? In one study of 8 pro men's doubles matches, about 7 in 10 shots were played from what the researchers called the transition zone, roughly mid-court. [S13]
The 4.0 checklist asks you to block hard volleys, start making resets, and know which balls you can attack. And serves, returns and volleys now need 9 out of 10. [S5]
About 15% of DUPR players are rated 4.0 to 4.99. [S11]
The drill: the mid-court reset. Stand mid-court. Your partner at the kitchen line feeds balls at a pace you can control, aimed below your waist. Block them softly into the kitchen. 3 sets of 10, forehand and backhand. Only speed up once you're landing 9 out of 10. [S5]
This drill gets faster and brings the ball near your face, so wear eye protection rated for racket sports. [S18][S19]
The mistake? Swinging at every hard ball. At this level, you block it, drop it, and wait for a better ball. [S1][S6]

## Level 5: No Weak Spots
Level 5 is 5.0 and above. These players have mastered the dink and the drop, change pace easily, and rarely make unforced errors. [S1][S7]
Every shot works from both the forehand and the backhand. [S7]
Think of a castle with no weak wall. Whichever side you attack, it holds.
Only about 1% of DUPR players are rated 5.0 or higher. [S11]
Drill 1: weak-side week. 10 minutes per session of backhand-only dinks and drops, 3 sessions a week. [S5][S7]
Drill 2: the error tally. Ask a friend to make a tick mark for every unforced error you make in one game, just like the official rating sheet. Then train the shot with the most ticks. [S3][S7]
Strength train all your major muscle groups at least 2 days a week, and start light if you're new to it. [S22] If something already hurts, like your calf, Achilles, knee or shoulder, see a physio or sports doctor before adding load.
In a review of sports trials, strength training cut injuries to about a third of the usual rate. That came from only a handful of trials, none in pickleball. [S21][S26]
The mistake? Chasing power. The top level is defined by control and variety. Pace is just one of the tools. [S1][S7]

## Stay on court
In US emergency-room data, the most common way people got hurt playing pickleball was falling, and most of those patients were over 60. [S14][S15]
So when a lob sails over your head, turn and run. Don't backpedal. [S20]
For players aged 18 to 34 in that data, the most common cause of injury was a twist or a rolled ankle. In sports trials, balance training lowered injury rates. [S14][S21]
A ball strike is the most common cause of pickleball eye injuries, so eye protection rated for racket sports is worth wearing at any level. [S18][S19]
And stop and get checked promptly if you feel a sudden kick or snap at the back of your heel or calf, or can't push off your toes. [S27]
Same if a hit to the eye leaves you with blurred vision, flashes or pain, if a fall leaves your wrist swollen or misshapen, if you hit your head, or if a rolled ankle won't take your weight. [S18][S14][S16][S20]

## Your plan
So, which level are you? Count your misses. [S3]
Can't land 7 serves out of 10? You're probably still on Level 1. [S3]
Landing 7, but stuck at the back? Level 2: return deep and get forward. [S4]
Getting forward, but always rushed? Level 3: the drop. [S4]
Caught in mid-court a lot? Level 4: the reset. [S5]
It's a rough self-check, not an official rating. For that, a rater watches your whole game. [S2][S3]
This week, play 2 sessions. Warm up, do one 10-ball test, then spend 15 minutes on your level's drill.

## Close
Remember that ball you smashed straight back at your feet? Next time, hit it soft, and walk in behind it.
