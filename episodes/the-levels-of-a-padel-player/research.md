## Promise
Find out which padel level you are really at, from learning how the walls work to playing tournaments, and the one skill that moves you up to the next level.

## How the rating systems actually work

### Playtomic (0–7), the app most club players see
- **Scale:** 0 to 7, with 7 the highest [S5]. Amateurs can reach at most **5.6** [S4].
- **Starting number:** a one-time onboarding questionnaire gives an estimate [S3]. A coach-run levelling session at a certified club assesses "strokes, focus, competitiveness and condition", and resets your reliability to 50% [S4].
- **How it moves:** "Levels are calculated based on match results and opponent rankings" [S5]. According to the Help Center excerpts, after each *competitive* match the system averages each team's level, compares the expected result with the actual one, and adjusts. It considers your opponents' levels, your partner's level and your **reliability**. Beating stronger opponents gains more, and friendly/casual matches don't count [S3].
- **Reliability:** a confidence score. It's low for new players, so their level swings more. It rises as you play competitive matches, and users can't set it [S3].
- **Why it confuses people (explainer logic):** your number depends on *who you played with and against* and on how sure the app is about you, not only on whether you won [S3][S5]. So you can win and barely move, or move a lot after a single match early on. The exact formula isn't published [S3].
- **Band labels (excerpt-only, verify before use):** <1 Initiation · 1.0–1.49 Beginner · 1.5–2.4 Initiation Intermediate · 2.5–3.4 Intermediate · 3.5–4.4 Intermediate High · 4.5–5.4 Intermediate Advanced · ~5.4–5.6 Competition [S3].

### LTA Padel Rating (1.0–7.0), self-assessed by statement
The nicest skill descriptors of any system. You pick the sentence that sounds like you [S2]:
- 1.0 "still working on getting the ball in play" · 2.0 "can get the ball in play but my shots lack direction and control" · 2.5 "can rally with basic shots but I lack technique" · **3.0 "pretty consistent and can use tactics like playing off the wall"** [S2]
- 3.5 "lack technique but do have some control" (competent tennis/squash players new to padel land here) · 4.0 "quite controlled… can cover it well… basic understanding of padel tactics" · 4.5 "very good at covering the court… good understanding of padel tactics" · **5.0 "an experienced tournament player"** [S2]
- 5.5 nationally ranked · 6.0–7.0 tied to professional world rankings (7.0 = inside the top 150 men / top 50 women) [S2]

### FIP
FIP sets the rules (court, serve, walls, scoring) [S1] but I found no FIP amateur skill-level scale. Other apps and countries use their own scales (Playtomic's blog shows a generic 1–10 chart and notes "different organizations have their own ranking systems") [S5]. **Takeaway line:** "The numbers differ by app. The skills don't."

## Framework: the 5-level ladder (lineup of 5 mascots)

| Level | Our name | ≈ Playtomic | ≈ LTA | What they can do | One skill that moves you up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Wall Dodger** | 0–1.5 | 1.0–2.0 | Getting the ball in play; underarm serve [S2][S7] | **Let it come off the back glass** (read the rebound) [S2][S11] |
| 2 | **The Rallier** | 1.5–2.5 | 2.5 | Rallies with basic shots, little technique [S2] | **Short swing + move as a pair** (both up or both back) [S11] |
| 3 | **The Wall Player** | 2.5–3.5 | 3.0–3.5 | Consistent, plays off the wall [S2] | **The lob to win the net** [S10][S11] |
| 4 | **The Net Keeper** | 3.5–4.5 | 4.0–4.5 | Controlled, covers court, knows basic tactics [S2] | **Bandeja** to keep the net when lobbed (then víbora) [S8][S9] |
| 5 | **The Tournament Player** | 4.5–5.6 | 5.0–5.5 | Tournament player, very good tactics [S2]; amateur ceiling 5.6 on Playtomic [S4] | **Shot choice under pressure:** finish only the right balls (smash/x3), chiquita, fewer errors on big points [S8][S12][S13] |

**Hero thread (custom scene idea):** padel is a fight for the **net**. In pro finals, attacking shots were ~65% volleys, ~23% bandejas and ~12% smashes. Winners made at least one attacking shot in 85% of points, while losers made **none** in 47% of points [S12]. Each level up is one more tool for *getting to* the net (lob) or *staying* there (bandeja), plus knowing when to finish. Visual: a court in side view with a "net control" meter. The mascot climbs from the back glass to the net, level by level.

**Lineup acting:** L1 ducks as the ball rebounds off the back glass, then swings at air. L2 has a steady cross-court rally with a counter. L3 lobs high over two opponents and runs forward. L4 does a bandeja ("tray") at eye height and steps back in to the net. L5 lets one ball go to the glass calmly, then smashes a short ball (x3), with a trophy.

### Rules primer (Level 1 scenes)
- **Court:** 20 × 10 m inside, enclosed. Back walls are 3 m wall + 1 m mesh = 4 m. Service lines are 6.95 m from the net. The net is 0.88 m at the centre and 0.92 m at the ends [S1].
- **Serve:** one foot behind the service line. Bounce the ball first, then hit it **at or below waist height**, with at least one foot on the ground, diagonally into the opponent's box. You get two serves [S1][S7]. If the serve bounces in the box and then hits the **metal fence** before the second bounce, it's a fault [S1]. The receiver must let the serve bounce [S1].
- **Walls (the big one):**
  - Your shot must land on the opponents' **floor first**. If it hits their wall or fence before bouncing, you lose the point [S1][S7].
  - After one bounce, the ball can hit any wall or fence and is **still live** until it bounces a second time [S1][S7].
  - You may hit the ball into **your own** wall so that it rebounds over the net, as long as it then lands in their court [S1].
  - Touching the net loses the point [S1]. If the ball bounces in their court and then flies out over the walls, it counts as in, and where courts have a legal safety area players may run outside to play it [S1][S7].
- **Scoring:** 15-30-40, sets to 6 with a tie-break at 6-6, best of three [S1]. Many events use a **golden point** (one deciding point at deuce, receivers choose the side) or the newer **Star Point** (a deciding point after the third deuce). All are official options [S1][S8].
- **Racket wrist cord is obligatory** [S1].

## Level 1: The Wall Dodger (≈ Playtomic 0–1.5 / LTA 1.0–2.0)

**Explanation.** LTA 1.0 is "still working on getting the ball in play", and 2.0 "can get the ball in play but my shots lack direction and control" [S2]. The new thing for anyone from tennis is the glass. After the bounce, a ball that hits your back glass is still in play and comes back to you [S1][S7]. Beginners usually rush to hit it before the glass, or get caught by the rebound (coach observation; the LTA's 3.0 descriptor makes "playing off the wall" the marker of the next tier [S2]).

**Analogy.** A friend passing to you off a wall in the playground. Wait for the bounce back and the ball comes to you, slower and easier.

**Sport moments.** The mascot faces the net, the ball sails past and bounces, hits the back glass and smacks the mascot in the back (comic). Replay: the mascot turns sideways, lets it pass, waits, and hits it gently after the rebound.

**What moves you up: reading the back glass.** The LTA advises letting the ball hit the glass after the bounce to buy time. Prepare the racket early and take pace off the shot [S11].

**Training methods + dosage (coach-derived; skill from [S2][S11]).**
1. **Toss-and-turn (no racket first).** Your partner stands at the net and tosses the ball so it bounces once and hits your back glass. You turn side-on, let it come off, and catch it after the rebound. Do 3 × 10, 2 sessions/week. Progression: catch → hit softly back over the net → partner feeds with the racket → mix of hard and soft feeds.
2. **Serve basics: 10-serve count.** Bounce, hit at or below the waist, and aim diagonally into the box [S1]. Do 10 from each side, 3 rounds. Goal: 7/10 in (target coach-derived; no official padel % benchmark).
3. **Rules walk-through.** Play slow points calling out "floor first, then wall" for every shot [S1][S7].

**Not suitable / caution:** very low risk. Watch the ball, not the glass. Don't step backwards fast near the walls (coach-derived). Wear the wrist cord, which is obligatory [S1].

**Common mistakes.**
- Hitting your shot straight into the opponents' glass. It's out unless it bounces first [S1][S7].
- Swinging tennis-style at everything. The LTA recommends a short backswing on both sides [S11].

## Level 2: The Rallier (≈ Playtomic 1.5–2.5 / LTA 2.5)

**Explanation.** "Can rally with basic shots but I lack technique" [S2]. Pairs often get split, one at the net and one at the back. The LTA describes the normal shapes as "up at the net with your partner or both towards the back of the court defending" [S11].

**Analogy.** Two windscreen wipers tied together: move side to side and forward or back as one unit.

**Sport moments.** Split pair: one mascot at the net, one at the back, and a ball goes through the gap between them (cross mark). Fixed: both mascots slide forward together, then back together.

**What moves you up: consistency + pair positioning.** The 3.0 descriptor is "pretty consistent" [S2]. The LTA's beginner drills are a half-court rally with a consecutive-shot target, and moving with your partner after deep shots [S11].

**Training methods + dosage.**
1. **Cross-court count.** Half-court diagonal rally. Score the longest streak. Do 3 × 3 min. Goals: 10, then 20 in a row [S11] (numbers coach-derived).
2. **Mirror shadowing.** No ball. A coach points "up" or "back" and both partners move together. Do 4 × 30 s (coach-derived; "move across the court with your partner" [S11]).
3. **Short-swing volley wall.** At home, volley against an outside wall [S11]. Do 3 × 1 min (coach-derived). Keep the backswing short [S11].

**Not suitable / caution:** low risk. Call "mine/yours" to avoid partner collisions. Communicating is an LTA beginner skill [S11]; collision prevention is coach-derived.

**Common mistakes.**
- One up, one back. Not one of the two standard shapes [S11].
- Hitting hard to "win". The LTA says padel is about accuracy and placement more than power ([S11] coaching emphasis; see Unverified for the exact quote).

## Level 3: The Wall Player (≈ Playtomic 2.5–3.5 / LTA 3.0–3.5)

**Explanation.** "Pretty consistent and can use tactics like playing off the wall" [S2]. Typical weakness: getting stuck at the back, defending forever. The LTA calls the lob "the most important shot in padel". It pushes the net team back and gives you time to move forward [S10].

**Analogy.** A rainbow over the castle wall. You can't go through the defenders, so you go over them and walk in the front gate.

**Sport moments.** Both mascots are pinned at the back. One lobs high and cross-court over the net pair, the opponents turn and run back, and our pair walks forward to the net.

**What moves you up: the lob to take the net.** Use good height so it goes over their heads and comes off the glass less. Aim cross-court for more room, landing "just beyond the service line but before the back glass". Racket path low to high, legs bent [S10].

**Training methods + dosage.**
1. **Racket-path ladder (LTA).** Balance the ball on the racket face → flick it up → drop and hit it up → tap-up rallies → full lob [S10]. Do 5 min per session (dose coach-derived).
2. **Two up, two back (LTA).** The baseline pair starts every point with a lob, then the point is played out. Swap every two points, first to 10 [S10].
3. **Target lobs.** A cone zone between the service line and the back glass, cross-court. Do 3 × 10 lobs and count the hits (target zone from [S10]; reps coach-derived).

**Not suitable / caution:** low risk. The receiving side of lob drills will be turning and running back, so see Level 4 caution.

**Common mistakes.**
- A short lob. It lands in front of the service line and gets smashed (landing zone from [S10]; consequence is coaching logic).
- Lobbing and then staying at the back. The point of the lob is to move forward [S10][S11].

## Level 4: The Net Keeper (≈ Playtomic 3.5–4.5 / LTA 4.0–4.5)

**Explanation.** "Quite controlled on the court, can cover it well… basic understanding of padel tactics", rising to "very good at covering the court" at 4.5 [S2]. Now *you* hold the net and the opponents lob you. The answer is the **bandeja**: "the most common overhead in padel… known as the antidote to the lob". It's a defensive overhead that keeps your net position [S9]. It's named after the Spanish for "tray" and hit with slice, deep, "so it stays low off the glass" [S8].

**Bandeja vs víbora vs smash (beat the writer can use):**
| Shot | What it is | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Bandeja** | Open face like a tray, contact at about eye height, racket comes *across* the body, underspin, not much pace [S8][S9] | Keep the net, buy time [S9] |
| **Víbora** ("viper") | Attacking overhead at head/shoulder height with **side-spin** [S8] | Pressure, a low awkward bounce off the glass (coaching consensus; see Unverified) |
| **Smash / bajada / rulo** | Flat, fast smash [S9]. Bajada: aggressive downward shot from above head height. Rulo: lower-risk topspin smash toward the fence [S8] | Finish |
| **Por tres (x3)** | "Kick smash, usually played from close to the net", which bounces and escapes over the 3 m side wall [S8] | Winner (Level 5) |
| **Por cuatro (x4)** | Smash that leaves over the 4 m back wall/fence (definitions vary; not in LTA glossary) | Winner (Level 5); see Unverified |

In pro men's finals, bandejas were ~23% of attacking shots against ~12% for smashes [S12]. So even at the top, the controlled overhead is used about twice as often as the big smash.

**Analogy.** Carrying a tray of drinks: smooth, flat, controlled, so nothing spills. The smash is throwing the tray.

**Sport moments.** An opponent lobs. The mascot turns sideways, racket up like a tray, hits a bandeja at eye height across the body, then steps forward and finishes level with the partner at the net [S9].

**What moves you up (to 5): tactics and finishing.** Next comes "experienced tournament player… very good understanding of padel tactics" [S2].

**Training methods + dosage.**
1. **Shadow bandeja.** No ball. Turn, tray, contact at eye height, across the body, "scarf" finish, step back in [S9]. Do 3 × 10 (coach-derived).
2. **Defend the net (LTA).** Two players at the net, two at the back. The back pair lobs, and the net pair must hit a bandeja landing beyond the service line, then the point is live. Swap every two points, first to 10 [S9]. Progression (coach-derived): hand-fed soft lobs → racket-fed lobs → live lobs → add a víbora when the lob is short.
3. **Bandeja-to-net recovery.** After each bandeja, step forward to the net and split step on the next ball. Do 3 × 8 reps with 60 s rest (coach-derived; "use the momentum to move back toward the net" [S9]).

**Not suitable / caution:** **This is the injury level.** The typical padel calf tear ("tennis leg") happens in "the transition from single-leg landing from an overhead shot to try to volley the next shot" [S15]. Build calf strength first (see Safety). If you're new to overheads, keep feeds short in volume (coach-derived). Players with current shoulder pain should get it checked before adding overhead volume (general caution).

**Common mistakes.**
- Smashing every lob. The bandeja avoids "a lot of pace and power" so you have time to recover [S9].
- Letting the racket finish down by your feet like a tennis serve. In the bandeja it comes *across* the body [S9].
- Retreating to the back after the overhead instead of stepping back in [S9].

## Level 5: The Tournament Player (≈ Playtomic 4.5–5.6 / LTA 5.0–5.5)

**Explanation.** LTA 5.0 is "an experienced tournament player who has a very good understanding of padel tactics", and 5.5 is nationally ranked [S2]. On Playtomic, 5.6 is the amateur ceiling [S4]. The jump here is decision-making: when to attack, when to reset, and making fewer errors when it matters. Even pros make more errors than winners. In men's pro golden points, over 60% end in an error [S13].

**Tools at this level:**
- **Chiquita:** a soft shot from the back to the feet of the net players, forcing them to volley up [S8]. Playtomic lists lobs, chiquitas and bandejas plus "effective use of walls for both attack and defense" as advanced skills [S5].
- **Finishing:** x3 kick smash near the net [S8]. Winners in pro finals used smashes more than losers [S12].
- **Nevera ("the fridge"):** targeting the weaker opponent [S8].

**Analogy.** A chess player: every shot sets up the next.

**Sport moments.** Golden point, crowd tension, the mascot breathes. It lets a fast ball go to the glass, plays a chiquita at the opponents' feet, they pop it up, and it hits an x3 out over the side wall. Trophy.

**Training methods + dosage (coach-derived).**
1. **Golden-point games.** Play every game to a single deciding point at deuce [S1]. One set per session. Track errors on deciding points (numbers coach-derived; rationale [S13]).
2. **Attack-or-reset rule game.** The net pair may only smash balls that land in front of the service line. Anything deep must be a bandeja/víbora. Play to 10 (coach-derived).
3. **Chiquita-volley-up game.** The back pair must play a chiquita at the feet [S8]. The net pair scores double for a winning volley *below net height* (coach-derived).
4. **Athletic base (levels 3–5).** Train all major muscle groups ≥2 days/week and build gradually; for strength ~80% 1RM, 2–3 sets [S20]. Strength training cut sports injuries to about a third in RCTs (RR 0.315) and balance training to about half (RR 0.55), but stretching didn't (RR 0.96). Not padel-specific [S19]. Menu (coach-derived): calf raises (straight and bent knee), single-leg balance, split squats, lateral lunges, rows, rotator-cuff band work, wrist-extensor work.

**Not suitable / caution:** more matches per week means more load. In one Spanish club sample, more weekly match hours and more years played were associated with injury [S17]. Increase weekly playing time gradually [S15].

**Common mistakes.**
- Going for a winner on every high ball. In pro play the bandeja is used about twice as often as the smash [S12].
- Ignoring big points. Golden points are decided mostly by errors [S13].

## Myths

1. **"My Playtomic level = my skill."** It's a results-based estimate that depends on opponents, partner and reliability, and only competitive matches count [S3][S5]. The LTA's skill statements describe what you can *do* [S2].
2. **"I won, so my level should go up a lot."** Gains depend on the expected result. Beating stronger opponents counts more, and low reliability makes numbers swing [S3].
3. **"Never let the ball hit the glass."** After one bounce, the glass is legal, and using it buys time [S1][S11]. "Playing off the wall" is literally the LTA 3.0 marker [S2].
4. **"Hitting your own wall is illegal."** You can play the ball off your own wall as long as it then lands on their floor [S1].
5. **"Smash every lob."** The bandeja is the standard answer to the lob [S9]. Pros hit about twice as many bandejas as smashes in attack [S12].
6. **"Serve overarm like tennis."** The serve must be after a bounce and at or below waist height [S1][S7].
7. **"Padel is gentle, so no need to prepare."** The elbow is the most injured site, tendon and muscle injuries dominate [S14], and the typical calf tear comes from landing an overhead and sprinting in [S15].
8. **"Static stretching prevents injuries."** No effect in RCT pooling (RR 0.96), while strength and balance training did reduce injuries [S19].
9. **"Experienced players don't get hit in the eye."** Experience doesn't reduce eye-injury risk, possibly because of higher ball speeds [S16].

## Safety (for the script and the fact-checker)

- **General:** this is education for healthy amateurs, not medical advice. Anyone with current pain should see a physio or sports doctor before adding load.
- **Elbow (tennis elbow):** the most common padel injury site [S14], and over 20% of injuries in one review [S15]. It causes pain on the outside of the elbow, worse with gripping and twisting [S21]. Choose racket weight and grip size to suit you [S15]. In a club sample, 350–370 g rackets were associated with more muscle/tendon/ligament injuries (association only) [S17]. **Red flag:** if pain continues after a few days of rest, see a GP [S21].
- **Calf and Achilles:** "tennis leg" (medial calf tear) is the most typical padel muscle injury, usually when landing on one leg after an overhead and pushing off to volley [S15]. Achilles tendinopathy is also frequent [S15]. Prevention focus: ankle mobility and calf/Achilles strength [S15][S19]. **Red flags:** sudden heel/calf pain, a pop or snap, feeling "hit in the back of the leg", or being unable to push off or rise onto tiptoe. Stop and get seen [S23].
- **Knee/ankle:** knee and ankle/foot were the top sites in a 16–40-relevant club sample (mean age 30) [S17]. Balance training lowers injury risk in RCTs (not padel) [S19]. Shoes: herringbone/mixed soles were associated with more severe injuries in that sample (association only) [S17]. Shoes should suit the court carpet [S15]. Sand-heavy courts were also associated with injury [S17].
- **Shoulder/back/neck:** the shoulder is a common site [S14][S16]. Neck pain is reported more in left-side players and back pain in right-side players [S16]. Overhead volume should build gradually (coach-derived).
- **Eyes:** padel carries high eye-injury risk because of the small ball and high speed, and experience doesn't protect you [S16]. Three Swedish cases all needed surgery, and smash speeds reach ~130 km/h [S18]. Recommend sports goggles with **polycarbonate** lenses [S18][S24]. Note: ASTM F3164 names tennis, squash, racquetball and pickleball, **not padel** [S24]. Coaching tip (coach-derived): at the net, don't turn to watch your partner's shot from the back. Keep your eyes forward or racket up. **Red flags (A&E):** any change in vision after a hit, a hit at high speed, headache, light sensitivity, nausea, or being unable to open or move the eye [S22].
- **Walls and out-of-court play:** only run outside the court where the venue has the legal safety area (≥3 m wide × 4 m long) [S1]. Glass is unforgiving, so don't backpedal into it (coach-derived).
- **Wrist cord:** compulsory under the rules, and protects others [S1].
- **Warm-up:** the rules include a 3-minute courtesy rally [S1], which is not enough on its own (coach-derived). I found **no padel-specific warm-up trial**. Suggested routine (coach-derived): 6–8 min of easy jog, side shuffles, carioca, leg swings, walking lunges, low pogo hops, calf raises, arm circles and band external rotations. Then 3–5 min of volleys and groundstrokes building to overheads last.
- **Load:** amateurs get hurt from poor preparation and rapidly increasing hours [S15]. More weekly match hours were associated with injury [S17]. Coach-derived rule: add no more than one extra session per week at a time.
- **No treatments, braces or supplements** are recommended.
