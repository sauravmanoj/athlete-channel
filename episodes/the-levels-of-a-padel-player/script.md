# The levels of a padel player
Promise: find out which padel level you're really at — from learning how the walls work to playing tournaments — and the one skill that moves you up to the next level.
Framework: five levels — Wall Dodger, Rallier, Wall Player, Net Keeper, Tournament Player — joined by one thread: the fight for the net.

## Hook
The ball flies past you, hits the back glass, and smacks you in the back. Point lost.
In padel, the glass is not your enemy. It's your friend. And learning that is just level 1. [S1][S2]
Today: the five levels of padel, which one you're really at, and the one skill that moves you up.

## The five levels
First, the numbers. Playtomic, a booking app a lot of club players use, rates you from 0 to 7. Amateurs top out at 5.6. [S4][S5]
It comes from match results: who you played with, who you played against, and how sure the app is about you. So you can win and barely move. [S3][S5]
The LTA's padel rating works differently. From 1.0 to 7.0, you pick the sentence that sounds like you. [S2]
The numbers differ. The skills don't. So my five levels are built on skills, and the names are mine.
Level 1, the Wall Dodger. Level 2, the Rallier. Level 3, the Wall Player. Level 4, the Net Keeper. And Level 5, the Tournament Player.
And there's a hidden thread. Padel is a fight for the net. In one study of four men's pro finals, the winning pair played at least one attacking shot in 85% of points. The losing pair played none in 47% of points. [S12]
Every level up gives you one more tool to win the net, or keep it.

## Level 1: The Wall Dodger
On the LTA scale, the 1.0 player is still working on getting the ball in play. [S2]
Rule one: your shot must land on the floor of the other side first. If it hits their glass before it bounces, you lose the point. [S1][S7]
Rule two: after one bounce, the ball can hit any wall, and it's still in play until it bounces again. [S1][S7]
The serve is underarm. Bounce the ball, then hit it at or below your waist, diagonally into the box. [S1][S7]
Think of a wall passing the ball back to you. Wait, and it comes to you, slower and easier.
Before any drill in this video, warm up for 6 to 8 minutes: easy jogging, side shuffles, leg swings, lunges, calf raises and arm circles. Then volleys and groundstrokes, overheads last.
New to padel, or back after a break? Build up your court time week by week, and don't play through pain.
To move up, read the back glass. Prepare your racket early, let the ball come off the glass, and take the pace off it. [S11]
The drill: toss and turn. Your partner tosses a ball that bounces once and hits your back glass. Turn side-on, let it come off, and catch it. 3 sets of 10, twice a week. Then swap the catch for a soft shot. [S11]
The mistake? Swinging like a tennis player. Keep your backswing short on both sides. [S11]

## Level 2: The Rallier
The LTA 2.5 player can rally with basic shots, but lacks technique. [S2]
The Rallier trap: one partner at the net, one at the back. The ball goes straight through the gap.
The LTA's two normal shapes: both up at the net, or both back defending. [S11]
Think of two windscreen wipers tied together. You move as one.
To move up: be consistent, and move as a pair. [S2][S11]
Drill 1: the cross-court count. Rally diagonally in half the court and count your longest streak. 3 rounds of 3 minutes. Aim for 10 in a row, then 20. [S11]
Drill 2: mirror shadowing. No ball. A coach calls "up" or "back", and you both move together. 4 rounds of 30 seconds. [S11]
Call "mine" or "yours" on balls down the middle, so you don't clash rackets. [S11]
The mistake? Hitting hard to win the point. Placement beats power here. [S11]

## Level 3: The Wall Player
At LTA 3.0, you're pretty consistent and can use tactics like playing off the wall. [S2]
The problem? You're stuck at the back, defending, while the other pair owns the net.
The way out is the lob. The LTA calls it the most important shot in padel. It pushes the net pair back and buys you time to move in. [S10]
Picture a rainbow over a castle wall. You can't go through the defenders, so you go over them.
Hit it high, cross-court, landing just past the service line and before the back glass. Bend your legs and swing low to high. [S10]
The drill: two up, two back. The pair at the back starts every point with a lob, then the point is played out. Swap every 2 points. First pair to 10. [S10]
If you're being lobbed, turn side-on and run back, but stop short of the glass. Let the lob bounce and come off the wall, then play it. One player chases, the other calls it. [S1][S11]
The mistake? Hitting a great lob, then staying at the back. The lob is your ticket to the net. Use it. [S10][S11]

## Level 4: The Net Keeper
At LTA 4.0, you're controlled, you cover the court well, and you know basic padel tactics. [S2]
Now you hold the net, so the other pair lobs you. Your answer is the bandeja. [S9]
Bandeja is Spanish for "tray". It's an overhead with an open face, hit at about eye height, across your body, with slice. It's not meant to win the point. It keeps you at the net. [S8][S9]
Think of carrying a tray of drinks. Smooth and flat, so nothing spills. A smash is throwing the tray.
In those pro finals, bandejas were about 23% of attacking shots. Smashes, only about 12%. [S12]
Its attacking cousin is the víbora, or "viper": an overhead hit around head or shoulder height, with side-spin. [S8]
Drill 1: shadow bandeja. No ball. Turn sideways, racket up like a tray, swing across your body at eye height, then step back in to the net. 3 sets of 10. [S9]
Drill 2: defend the net. 2 at the net, 2 at the back. The back pair lobs. The net pair must land a bandeja past the service line, then play it out. Swap every 2 points. First to 10. Start with soft hand-fed lobs, and go live once most of your bandejas land deep. [S9]
Before you start, careful: the typical padel calf tear happens when you land on one leg after an overhead and push off to volley. [S15]
So first, build your calves: calf raises 2 or 3 times a week, straight-knee and bent-knee, 3 sets of 10 to 15, building to one leg. Keep early overhead sessions to about 10 minutes, and land on both feet when you can. If your shoulder or calf already hurts, see a physio first. [S15][S19]
The mistake? Smashing every lob. Then backing off to the baseline. Hit the bandeja, and step back in. [S9]

## Level 5: The Tournament Player
LTA 5.0 is an experienced tournament player with a very good understanding of padel tactics. [S2]
The jump isn't a new shot. It's choosing the right one under pressure.
The por tres, or x3: a kick smash from close to the net that bounces out over the side wall. [S8]
And the big points. With golden point scoring, deuce is settled by one deciding point. [S1] In men's pro golden points, more than 60% ended in an error, not a winner. [S13]
Drill 1: golden-point sets. Play every deuce as one deciding point, and count your errors. 1 set per session. [S1][S13]
Drill 2: attack or reset. The net pair may only smash short lobs they can hit without stepping back past the service line. Anything that pushes them back must be a bandeja or a víbora. Play to 10. [S8][S9]
Strength train all your major muscle groups at least 2 days a week. Start light, build up gradually, and if something already hurts, see a physio first. [S20]
In a review of sports trials, strength training cut injury rates to about a third of normal, and balance training to about half. Stretching didn't help. Only a few trials tested strength work, and none were in padel. [S19]
The mistake? Going for a winner on every high ball. Pick your moment. [S9][S12]

## Stay on court
In a review of padel injury studies, the elbow was the most commonly injured spot. Tennis elbow alone made up more than 20% of injuries in another review. [S14][S15]
Pick a racket weight and grip size that suit you. If your outer elbow still hurts after a few days' rest, see a doctor. [S15][S21]
The ball is small and fast, and experience doesn't protect your eyes. Sports goggles with polycarbonate lenses are worth wearing at any level. [S16][S18][S24] And at the net, don't turn round to watch your partner's shot.
Stop and get checked if you feel a sudden pop or snap at the back of your calf or heel, or can't push up onto your toes. [S23]
If the ball hits you in the eye, stop playing and get it checked by a doctor the same day. Go to the emergency room straight away if your vision changes, light hurts, you feel sick, or you can't open or move the eye. [S22][S18]

## Your level, this week
So which are you? Dodging the glass? Level 1. Split from your partner? Level 2. Stuck at the back? Level 3. Smashing every lob? Level 4. Losing big points? Level 5.
This week: warm up, then do the drills for your level, twice.
Next time the ball flies past you into the back glass, don't flinch. Turn, wait, and let the wall pass it back.
