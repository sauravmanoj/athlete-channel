# Channel formula

This is a mascot-led explainer channel: one recognisable character teaches amateur athletes the *why* and *how* of performance — energy, speed, power, skill, recovery, mindset — in plain language, with concrete drills at the end of every section.

The model is the "illustrated character explains sports science" format. Learn from the formula, never copy another channel's character, colours, titles word-for-word, or thumbnails.

## Why it works
1. **The character is the brand.** Same mascot in every video and thumbnail → instant recognition in a feed. Use it in at least half the scenes.
2. **Desire-driven questions.** Every video promises an outcome the viewer already wants: never get tired, jump higher, be faster, stay calm, hit harder.
3. **Simple frameworks.** "Three energy systems", "the four levels", "five mistakes". A framework makes a 7-minute video feel organised and shareable.
4. **Science, made concrete.** Each idea ends in something to *do* (a drill, a rule, a routine) for specific sports.
5. **Templates × sports.** Once a title format works, it's repeated across sports — each sport is a new audience for a proven script.

## Proven title templates (rotate across sports and topics)

| Template | Example |
|---|---|
| How to never get tired in [sport] | How to never get tired in badminton |
| The physique of a [sport] athlete | The physique of a swimmer |
| Your [skill] is weak (here's why) | Your first step is slow (here's why) |
| How to [athletic outcome] faster than anyone | How to react faster than anyone |
| The levels of a [sport] player | The levels of a cricket batter |
| All [topic] explained | All types of football players explained |
| [N] mistakes killing your [outcome] | 5 mistakes killing your sprint speed |
| What happens to your body when you [habit] for 30 days | …play football every day for 30 days |
| The mental game of a [sport] athlete | The mental game of a boxer |
| How to [recover / sleep / eat] like an athlete | How to recover overnight |

Titles: lower friction, 4–8 words, no clickbait the video can't deliver. Avoid promises that aren't physiologically true ("grow taller at any age" — don't).

## Script structure (5–9 minutes ≈ 800–1,400 words at 160 wpm)

1. **Hook (0:00–0:20).** Picture the outcome or the pain: "Imagine finishing the third set and still feeling fresh." Then the promise: "Today I'll show you how to train so that happens." No channel intro.
2. **The framework (≈ 0:20–1:00).** Name the parts: "Your body has three engines…". This is the map of the video.
3. **One chapter per part (≈ 70%).** For each: what it is (one analogy), why it matters in real play (a sport moment), how to train it (2–3 concrete drills with sets/reps/rest when appropriate), a common mistake.
4. **Put it together (≈ 10%).** A simple weekly plan or decision rule.
5. **Close (≈ 5%).** One-line recap callback to the hook. Optional soft pointer to a related video. No "smash that like button".

Voice: second person ("you"), confident, friendly, zero jargon without an immediate plain-English translation. Short sentences. One idea per sentence.

## Visual grammar

| Narration is… | Scene |
|---|---|
| Describing the athlete doing something | `mascot` with the right pose / cycle (run, jump, swing) |
| Naming a concept | `slam` |
| Comparing levels, types, stages, phases | `lineup` |
| You vs opponent, before vs after | `versus` |
| Giving steps, drills, a plan | `list` |
| Comparing quantities | `chart` |
| One striking number | `stat` |
| Starting a chapter | `title` |
| Pointing at a body part / muscle | `mascot` + `callouts` |
| Energy, fatigue, heart rate | `gauges`, battery/heart icons |

Rules:
- Change the picture every 3–8 seconds. Motion matters: prefer pose changes and cycles over static holds.
- Every pose should *act out* the sentence (tired → `hands_knees`, fast → `sprint` cycle, idea → `point_up`).
- Never three of the same non-mascot kind in a row.
- 1–2 `custom` hero scenes per episode for the central idea (a diagram of the energy systems, a reaction-time timeline…).

## Accuracy and safety (non-negotiable)
- Every claim traces to a source in `sources.json`: position stands (ACSM, NSCA, IOC consensus statements), peer-reviewed studies and reviews, textbooks, national sports bodies.
- Drills must be safe for a healthy amateur; add a progression or caveat when load, plyometrics or heat are involved.
- No medical diagnosis or treatment advice beyond general education ("if it hurts, see a physio").
- No supplements or nutrition claims beyond the evidence; no body-shaming; physiques are about function, not looks.
